Aupair China from Latin America: What to Settle Before You Say Yes

By Au Pair Ren

A friendly video call can make a family on the other side of the world feel close. You meet the children, see the living room and start imagining your arrival. Before you agree to a stay, ask the family to walk you through an ordinary week. That conversation gives you something firmer to plan around than a warm first impression.

If you are exploring an Aupair China stay from Latin America, start with the household you would join. The city matters, but so do the people who will be home on a Tuesday afternoon and the language you will use when a plan changes.

Start with what the family actually wants

Describe your languages accurately. If Spanish is your first language, say so, then explain what you can comfortably do in English or Mandarin. A certificate and an everyday conversation answer different questions. A video call lets both sides hear how communication would work.

Ask which language the parents expect you to use with the children. A family interested in English practice may have a different arrangement in mind from one that wants Spanish conversation. Find out before discussing a start date. You can offer the skills you have without promising fluency you are still developing.

The same applies to childcare experience. Describe the ages of children you have spent time with and the tasks you handled. If your experience is with school-age children, ask what support you would have before agreeing to care for a younger child.

Put one school day on paper

Ask a parent to describe a recent school day from breakfast to bedtime. Who takes the children to school? When would you be with them? Does another adult usually come home in the afternoon? Work through the parts that would involve you and write down the expected hours.

Then ask what changes during school holidays or when a child is ill. If the proposed week includes language classes, check when you could attend and how you would get there. Keep travel time in the plan. A class across the city can take more of the afternoon than the lesson itself.

Time off needs its own conversation. Agree on which periods are free, how changes would be discussed and whether spending free time at home changes any expectations. Send a recap after the call and invite corrections.

Talk about home without making it an interview performance

Ask who lives in the home and who makes day-to-day decisions about the children. You may speak first with one parent but later share the household with grandparents or another caregiver. It helps to meet the adults you would regularly see.

Request a video view of the room intended for your stay. Discuss bathroom use, storage and when people enter the room. Bring up a food requirement or an important sleep routine while the family can still tell you whether it can accommodate it.

Give the family room to ask you questions too. Explain what you hope to learn in China and what an enjoyable day with children looks like for you. Specific examples make it easier to compare expectations on both sides.

Check the journey from your own country

Build your travel plan around your passport, departure city and actual route. Applicants from different Latin American countries do not necessarily have the same document requirements or flight options. Verify the documents and permitted purpose of your stay with the relevant official authorities before booking.

Keep a written breakdown of who would pay for the flight, insurance and any agreed classes. Confirm when each payment would happen and what applies if the stay ends early. Ask about transit requirements for the route you are considering as well as entry into China.

Choose video-call times using both locations, including the date. Leave enough time to talk without either side rushing through an important decision late at night.

Know whom you could contact after arrival

Ask what happens if an arrangement stops working. Who would you speak with first? Is there an advisor involved, and what does that service include? If you arrange a match directly, ask the same practical questions instead of assuming someone else has taken responsibility for them.

Keep your own copies of the agreed schedule, payment arrangements and important contacts. Discuss health preparation with a qualified professional when needed, and check the terms of the insurance you are considering. A family introduction cannot answer those questions for you.
