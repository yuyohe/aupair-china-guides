Aupair China: What Should You Ask About Your Room Before You Match?

By Au Pair Ren

Suppose a host family sends you a photograph of a tidy bedroom. There is a bed, a desk and a wardrobe. You can imagine putting your suitcase down. Then a few questions come to mind: does anyone else use the wardrobe? Will the children come in to play? Can you close the door and spend an evening on your own?

For an Aupair China match, ask about these details while you are still getting to know the family. A short conversation on a video call can tell you how the room would fit into everyday life.

See the room you would actually use

Ask a host parent to show you the bedroom during a video call. Check that it is the room planned for your stay, and ask whether it will stay yours throughout the dates you are discussing. If it is currently a guest room, ask what happens when relatives visit.

You can keep the request simple: "Could you show me the room I would use, including the storage space? Would I need to move to a different room at any point?" A family can answer these questions without giving you a tour of every private part of the home.

Talk about the door and who comes in

Check whether the door closes properly. Ask how the household handles knocking and waiting for an answer. If there is a lock, ask how it works, who has a key and how the family would handle an emergency.

Discuss the ordinary situations too. Who cleans the room? Would someone come in to collect laundry? What would a parent say if a child wanted to enter while you were resting? Explain the privacy you need and ask the parents to talk it through with everyone who shares the home.

Find out what is inside the wardrobe

A bedroom can also be the place where a family keeps spare bedding, winter coats or a printer. Ask how much space will be cleared for your clothes and belongings. If family items will remain, agree on when someone might need to collect them.

For example, you could ask: "Will this wardrobe be available for my things? If you need something stored here, could we arrange a time for you to get it?" Settle any changes before arrival, so neither side has to rearrange the room after a long journey.

Ask how the bathroom works on a normal day

If you would share a bathroom, ask who else uses it and where it is in relation to the bedroom. A bathroom down the hall and one reached through another bedroom create different routines. Find out whether there is a busy period before school or work.

Ask where you can leave toiletries, how laundry is handled and whom to tell when something needs fixing. If you need a particular arrangement, bring it up during the call. The family needs time to decide whether it can offer it.

Explain what time off looks like when you are at home

You may spend some free afternoons out and others in your room. Tell the family if you plan to study, call people back home or rest there. Ask whether the adults understand that being at home during agreed time off does not make you available for planned childcare.

An invitation to dinner or a walk can still be welcome. Discuss how you would decline when you have other plans. Parents can help children learn that a closed bedroom door sometimes means waiting until later to play.

Check the things you will use every day

Look for a place to sit comfortably if you study online. Ask about the internet connection you would use, the room temperature at night and whether curtains give you the darkness you need to sleep. Bring up noise if your sleep or study schedule makes it important.

Share needs that affect the match early. A photograph may show a desk, but you still need to ask whether it will remain available. The same goes for any equipment the family has offered to lend you.

Write down the arrangement you both agreed to

After the call, send a short recap and ask the family to correct anything you misunderstood. Include which room you would use, any shared storage and how entry to the room would work. Keep the bathroom arrangement and agreed time off in the same recap.

Here is sample wording to adapt: "The bedroom shown on our call will be mine for the dates we discussed. We agreed which shelves will be cleared before I arrive. Anyone who needs something from the room will ask first. We will also confirm bathroom use and the weekly schedule together." Only keep sentences that describe what you have actually agreed.

If the family cannot confirm an arrangement you need, discuss it before you make travel plans. Once you arrive, check the room together and raise any difference with the host parent you have agreed to contact.

Bring the room into your first conversation

Ask about the bedroom alongside the people who live there and the week you would spend together. Before confirming a match, both sides should have had time to ask questions and read the written recap.
