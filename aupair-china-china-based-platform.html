Aupair China: Finding a host family on a China-based platform

By Au Pair Ren

A family may be interested in your au pair profile and still struggle to explain what they are looking for in English. You might read a short introduction and be left wondering what a normal week in that household would actually look like.

At Au Pair Ren, Chinese families can write about their lives in Chinese, while applicants read the English version and follow up with questions. We are a China-based au pair matching platform at aupair.ren, with our origin server in mainland China.

Using the website from China

Some international websites can be slow or difficult to access inside mainland China. A parent looking through profiles on a phone needs a site they can return to easily. Ren's mainland hosting lets us focus on that experience while also serving applicants overseas.

Server location is only part of loading speed; the network route and your own connection matter too. For matching, the bilingual profiles are just as useful. A parent can describe a family routine in Chinese without having to shorten it to the English they know. You can then ask about any detail that is unclear.

A Shanghai family interested in picture books and games

One Shanghai family writes about sharing everyday English through picture books and games. The introduction also mentions respecting an au pair's free time and personal boundaries. The profile lists one child to care for, a flexible hosting duration and October 2026 as the preferred start.

If that sounds like a household you would enjoy, ask how those activities would fit into a normal afternoon and when your own time would begin.

A Wuxi host who enjoys crafts and cooking

A host in Wuxi describes a private room and freedom during personal time. The parent works with crafts and mentions sharing Chinese cooking. The profile lists one child to care for and a three-month stay starting in December 2026.

An applicant who enjoys making things could ask what the family likes doing together and how Mandarin classes would fit around the proposed schedule.

A Qingdao family planning a six-month stay

A family in Qingdao lists two children to care for, everyday English communication and a six-month hosting plan. In their introduction, they mention a separate bedroom and family outings. Those details give an applicant several things to ask about: the children's routines, which outings are optional and how private time works.

The preferred arrival month shown is September 2026, which has already passed. Ask whether the plan has changed. These three examples show what families have written, not a promise that a particular place is still available.

What to ask before agreeing to a stay

The directory lets you compare location, children's age ranges and hosting plans. Missing information is worth asking about. Get a written weekly schedule and discuss accommodation, pocket money and time off. Make sure there is room for your language study, and agree how either side could end the stay.

Online applicants start with Ren Open. Ren Select is a separate service for candidates referred by partner agencies after local screening. Use the platform's messaging system for your first conversations and decide when you feel ready to exchange private contact details.

To begin your Aupair China search, browse a few Chinese host-family profiles at aupair.ren. In your first message, refer to something the family has actually written. A question about a shared interest or the proposed daily routine gives you both something useful to talk about.

Au Pair Ren provides information matching, not visa, immigration or employment-intermediary services. Check the entry, stay and activity requirements that apply to you before arranging travel.
