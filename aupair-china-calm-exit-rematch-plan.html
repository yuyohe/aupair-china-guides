I Want to Leave My Host Family

A calm exit and rematch plan that puts immediate safety, documents, accommodation and better screening ahead of pressure to save every placement.

By Ann · September 3, 2026

Wanting to leave a host family is not failure. A match can end because of unsafe behavior, broken agreements, incompatible personalities, isolation, excessive duties or a gap between the arrangement described online and daily life.

For an aupair China placement, “rematch” is not a universal legal category and there is no single nationwide two-week rule. Platform support and immigration status are separate questions.

First question: Is it safe to stay tonight?
If there is violence, sexual behavior, threats, confinement or another immediate danger, move to a safe place if you can do so safely. In China, call 110 for police assistance and 120 for first aid. Contact a trusted person and your embassy or consulate when appropriate.

1. Record facts privately

Create a private timeline of agreed duties, actual hours, payment, privacy and accommodation problems, and earlier attempts to resolve them. Save relevant agreements and messages somewhere you control. Do not publish family messages, addresses or children's information online.

2. Use one structured conversation

When the situation is not an emergency, state three things: what was agreed, what is happening, and what must change. A neutral adviser or bilingual support person can help. If the match will end, confirm the proposed final date in writing.

Sample notice: “After careful thought, I do not believe this placement is the right fit. I would like to end the arrangement calmly and agree on a safe transition. My proposed final day is [date]. Please confirm the childcare handover, any allowance or expenses still due, return of property, collection of my belongings and where I can stay during the transition.”

3. Separate five handovers

  1. Childcare: define the last date and time of responsibility.
  2. Money: list unpaid allowance and agreed expenses; keep receipts.
  3. Property: return keys and household property against a simple checklist.
  4. Documents: maintain access to your passport and essential personal documents.
  5. Accommodation and immigration: decide where you will stay and separately ask a qualified source how the move affects your documents.

4. Register the new place of stay

Official Chinese guidance says hotels handle hotel accommodation registration. At a non-hotel address, the foreign national or the person providing accommodation generally needs to complete local registration within 24 hours of arrival.

A March 2026 online-registration pilot began in seven provincial-level regions and is intended to expand gradually. Check the local procedure rather than assuming online registration is available everywhere. NIA's 12367 service provides immigration consultation in Chinese and English.

5. Build a rematch brief, not a blame story

A future family needs enough information to assess fit, not the private archive of a dispute. Explain the practical mismatch, the step you took to address it, and the boundaries you need next time.

Before saying yes, confirm household members, primary childcare scope, exact weekly hours, included and excluded tasks, private room, allowance and expenses, time off, emergency support, and how the proposed activities relate to your document status.

6. Do not let urgency recreate the same mismatch

Interview more than once and speak to the adults who live in the home. Ask to see the room on live video. A temporary safe plan is better than accepting the first family because a deadline feels frightening.

What a platform can—and cannot—do

A responsible platform can preserve relevant account records, facilitate communication, introduce profiles and encourage better screening. It cannot guarantee that a family will cooperate, promise a replacement family, provide emergency housing in every case or determine immigration status.

The calm order: immediate safety; secure documents and accommodation; write the facts; define the handover; obtain current immigration guidance; then screen any new family carefully.
